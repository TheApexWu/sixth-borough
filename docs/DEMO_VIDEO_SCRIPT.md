# Demo Video Script — Sixth Borough

**Locked Apr 11 morning. ~90 seconds. Spine: cultural memory infrastructure on local hardware.**

## The thesis (one sentence, locked)

**Sixth Borough is cultural memory infrastructure that runs on the community's own hardware instead of someone else's cloud.**

Not a time machine. Not a tourism app. Not a gaming demo. **Infrastructure.** Internalize this word. It is what makes Antler, NVIDIA, and any civic-adjacent listener lean in.

The "time machine" line is a hook for the door, never the spine.

## The 90-second script

Sponsor verbatim phrases are **bolded** so you can spot them in rehearsal. Per `docs/STACK.md` and `PITCH_FRAMINGS.md` line 9, these earn the 15-point Spark Story score. Do not paraphrase them.

### [OPENING — 14s. Stand next to the GN100. Tap the case on the second sentence.]

> "Sixth Borough is New York City's memory, localized in one box.
>
> One **DGX Spark**. One **GB10 Grace Blackwell Superchip**. **128 gigabytes of unified memory**. **Running locally**. **No internet connection at runtime**."

### [THE CLAIM — 22s. Slow down. This is the load-bearing beat. Eye contact on the last sentence.]

> "Cultural memory is the most extractable resource a city has. Right now Google, Zillow, and OpenAI are extracting it for free. They flatten it. They surveil it. They charge per query. And they only know what got scraped.
>
> We built the opposite."

### [DEMO — 35s. Move to the screen. Don't narrate the click. Just click.]

> "August 11th, 1973. 1520 Sedgwick Avenue. The Bronx. The night Kool Herc invented hip-hop."

**[Click the pin for `bronx-1973-08-11-sedgwick`. Let the narration appear on screen. STAY SILENT for 8–12 seconds while judges read.]**

> "That text was generated by a 30-billion-parameter model from **NVIDIA's Nemotron model family**, running on this box, ten feet from you, in the last 30 seconds. The ghost is a low-poly mesh of the rec room. The photo is from the NYPL Milstein collection. Model, data, renderer — all local. Nothing left the room."

### [ROADMAP — 12s.]

> "The Bronx model belongs to the Bronx. Chinatown's belongs to Chinatown. Phase 2 grounds every narration in real NYPL and Cornell Hip Hop Collection archives. Phase 3 is community-owned forks."

### [CLOSE — 7s. Make eye contact. Slow down. Land it.]

> "We're not building a tourism app. We're building cultural memory infrastructure for a city that's tired of being summarized.
>
> Computational ghosts need local hardware."

## Delivery notes

1. **The hardware tap is the most important physical action.** When you say "one box," you must touch the GN100. That gesture is the entire thesis compressed into a movement. Practice it standing up.

2. **Do not talk over the silent read.** Every instinct will say to fill the silence after the click. Resist. The 8–12 seconds of judges reading the Nemotron output IS the demo. Your job is to stand there and let the room watch.

3. **Three lines need eye contact specifically:**
   - "We built the opposite."
   - "Nothing left the room."
   - "Tired of being summarized."

   Everything else is connective tissue.

4. **No em dashes in delivery.** When you read this, replace every dash with a full stop and a breath. "We built the opposite. [breath]" not "We built — the opposite."

5. **If Nemotron hangs at click time**, do not panic. Say:
   > "While that loads — this runs on the same hardware NVIDIA built for exactly this category of work. Local-first inference is the whole claim, and local-first means we own the latency too."

   That buys you ~8 seconds and reframes the wait as a feature.

6. **The verified narration text** for the rehearsal target (Apr 11 ~10:30 ET smoke test):
   > "You feel the bass thump through the cracked linoleum as the needle clicks between vinyl, the smell of sweat and cheap perfume mixing with the metallic scent of hallway lights flickering overhead. The crowd's sneakers squeak on the concrete, and the rhythm stretches, endless, until the brick wall outside seems to pulse with the break."
   >
   > Nemotron will produce a different but equally period-accurate variant on each run. Don't memorize this exact text. Memorize the *feel* so you know when the live output is on or off the rails.

Every particle on screen is a documented historical movement, on the record, in NYC Open Data." |

## If the demo breaks

**Most likely failure modes** in priority order:

1. **Tailscale flake** at venue — orchestrator unreachable from the demo machine. Mitigation: rehearse the demo on the GN100's local screen via HDMI as the primary path. Tailscale is the secondary path, not the primary.
2. **llama-server crash** — orchestrator returns HTTP 502. Mitigation: keep tmux `llama` visible in a second pane, restart with `scripts/start-llama-nano.sh` if it dies.
3. **Orchestrator restart needed** (e.g., to pick up the staged CORS middleware) — costs 30 sec. Do this BEFORE the demo slot, not during.
4. **Renderer crash** — Carson's Bevy app or James's WebGL fallback. Mitigation: have BOTH paths warm. The judges don't care which one renders, only that something does.

**Hard fallback**: a pre-recorded 30-second OBS capture of the working happy path, plus the script above as voiceover. Carson can record this in 5 minutes once the smoke test is green. Worth doing as insurance regardless of confidence.
