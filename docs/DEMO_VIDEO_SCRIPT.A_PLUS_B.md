## The 90-second script

Sponsor verbatim phrases are **bolded**. Per `docs/STACK.md` and the rubric, these earn the 15-point Spark Story score.

### [OPENING — 14s. Stand next to the GN100. Tap the case on the second sentence. UNCHANGED.]

> "Sixth Borough is New York City's memory, localized in one box.
>
> One **DGX Spark**. One **GB10 Grace Blackwell Superchip**. **128 gigabytes of unified memory**. **Running locally**. **No internet connection at runtime**."

### [THE CLAIM — 22s. UNCHANGED.]

> "Cultural memory is the most extractable resource a city has. Right now Google, Zillow, and OpenAI are extracting it for free. They flatten it. They surveil it. They charge per query. And they only know what got scraped.
>
> We built the opposite."

### [DEMO — 35s. UNCHANGED.]

> "August 11th, 1973. 1520 Sedgwick Avenue. The Bronx. The night Kool Herc invented hip-hop."

**[Click the pin for `bronx-1973-08-11-sedgwick`. Let the narration appear on screen. STAY SILENT for 8–12 seconds while judges read.]**

> "That text was generated by a 30-billion-parameter model from **NVIDIA's Nemotron model family**, running on this box, ten feet from you, in the last 30 seconds. The ghost is a low-poly mesh of the rec room. The photo is from the NYPL Milstein collection. Model, data, renderer — all local. Nothing left the room."

### [ROADMAP — 12s. **REWRITTEN.** This is where A+B enters the script.]

**Old version** (from `DEMO_VIDEO_SCRIPT.md`):
> "The Bronx model belongs to the Bronx. Chinatown's belongs to Chinatown. Phase 2 grounds every narration in real NYPL and Cornell Hip Hop Collection archives. Phase 3 is community-owned forks."

**New version (A+B):**
> "Same engine, two outputs. A tenant lawyer in the South Bronx pulls a 4-page receipt on this address — every owner, every violation, every eviction filing — in 30 seconds. A museum curator at NYPL feeds in a 1903 archival film and gets back a stylized clip with locally-generated captions for next month's exhibit. Same Nemotron. Same box. Same data spine. Two compliance-locked markets that can't use the cloud."

**Why the rewrite is worth it:** the old roadmap beat was abstract ("Phase 2... Phase 3..."). The new version names two concrete users with two concrete deliverables, both running on the same hardware the judges are looking at. It also carries Surface B (ComfyUI / archival reanimation) into the recorded video without breaking the 12-second budget. **Word count: 64 → 70 words. Still ~12 sec at 140 wpm.**

### [CLOSE — 7s. UNCHANGED.]

> "We're not building a tourism app. We're building cultural memory infrastructure for a city that's tired of being summarized.
>
> Computational ghosts need local hardware."

| **"Who's the customer?"** | "Two named users on one engine. **Sofia Reyes** is a tenant lawyer at Bronx Legal Services. She pulls a 4-page footnoted building biography on any NYC address in 30 seconds — every owner, every violation, every alteration, every eviction filing, every adjacent demolition — to build a holdover hearing that currently takes her paralegal 6 hours. **Dr. Alicia Vance** is a preservation researcher at the Landmarks Preservation Commission. She feeds an archival film and an LPC designation report into the box and gets back a stylized 90-second museum clip with locally-generated captions. Both users **must** run locally — Sofia for client confidentiality, Alicia because rights holders won't upload originals to a cloud service. We are the only stack that gives them open NYC data plus private document ingestion plus a 30B reasoning model on a $3,000 box. Pricing: $500-2000/mo per seat for the lawyer surface, $5-50K per project for the museum surface. Same engine. Two markets." |
| **"Could a city planner use this tomorrow?"** | "Tomorrow morning Sofia Reyes can build a Cross-Bronx Expressway displacement case with footnoted DOB and ACS citations in the time it took her to make coffee. Tomorrow afternoon Alicia Vance can commission a stylized clip of 1903 Ellis Island arrivals for next month's NYPL exhibit, captioned by Nemotron, footnoted to her own LPC report. The data is already public. We made it queryable, traceable, and compliance-locked to local hardware so neither workflow ever touches a cloud LLM." |
| **"Why local? Cloud is faster."** | "Cloud is faster until it isn't. Until your API key gets revoked. Until the model gets aligned away from the answers you need. Until your community's stories get used as training data for someone else's product. And cloud is **legally impossible** for both our users — Sofia can't upload privileged client files to OpenAI, Alicia can't upload rights-controlled archival originals to a third party. Local hardware is the only legally compliant way to do either workflow. The 128 GB of unified memory on the GB10 holds the 30B reasoning model AND the SD1.5 image diffusion model AND the open-data corpus AND the renderer state simultaneously, with no PCIe round trips. That's the Spark Story." |
| **"What's the ML story? Are you doing prediction?"** | "We deliberately don't predict. Predicting gentrification IS gentrification. Our thesis: **we don't predict the city, we trace what it cost to build it**. The ML is two NVIDIA models on one box: NVIDIA Nemotron-3 Nano 30B from the NVIDIA Nemotron model family doing extraction over court filings and writing captions over archival footage, plus Stable Diffusion 1.5 with a 3DRenderStyle LoRA via ComfyUI doing the visual restylization on the same GB10. Both models load into the same 128 GB unified memory. Neither one forecasts." |
| **"Why two models? Isn't that overengineered?"** *(NEW question for the second NVIDIA stack item)* | "Two models because we have two surfaces. Nemotron handles the language work — extraction over court filings for Sofia, caption generation for Alicia. ComfyUI plus SD1.5 plus the 3DRenderStyle LoRA handles the visual work — restylization of archival film into a coherent visual language for Alicia's museum clip. They share the GB10's 128 GB of unified memory. No team without unified memory could load both models plus a renderer plus the open-data corpus on a single box without copying tensors across a PCIe bus. **This is the use case the GB10 was designed for.**" |
| **"How does this scale?"** | "Horizontally, by neighborhood and by buyer. One box per community. The Bronx model belongs to the Bronx. We don't need a billion-parameter model trained on the whole world. We need a 30B model trained on a square mile, paired with a diffusion model for the visual layer, both running on a $3,000 box that any law firm or library can buy outright." |
