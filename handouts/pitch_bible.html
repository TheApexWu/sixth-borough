Sixth Borough -- Pitch Bible

Alex Wu | Spark Hack NYC | Sun Apr 12, 2 PM demo | 5-min skim

1. Identity

  • Alex Wu, 23, NYU CS/Data Science, from Queens.
  • "Sixth Borough" = "Sixth Sense." A metaphysical borough that lives in the hardware. Not a simulation. Not a prediction engine. A memory organ for the city.
  • Everyone in tech predicts the future: gentrification models, property forecasting, "smart city" dashboards. We went the other direction. The past is where the signal is. You can't understand displacement without tracing it. You can't understand hip-hop without seeing the bulldozer that created the density that created the rec room.
  • The past can be profitable AND meaningful. Legal aid attorneys need building histories. Insurance underwriters need demolition context. REIT analysts need ownership chains. Real buyer segments, not charity.
  • Cities are being optimized for the future at the expense of their own history. The Mamdani administration is reshaping NYC's urban policy right now. Before you rezone, you should know what you're erasing. Sixth Borough is the receipt. We want to bring this directly to the mayor's office -- give city government institutional memory before it makes irreversible decisions.
  • The inspiration was the PS2. Not nostalgia -- the hardware itself as an object of genuine preservation. A PS2 is timeless. You put it in a room, you put a disc in, and it holds an entire world. No updates, no cloud, no account. It's a vehicle of memory. The DGX Spark is the same idea at city scale. One box, one world, fully self-contained. The immersive feel of something you own and can touch. That's the design philosophy.
  • Carson built a native Bevy 0.18.1 renderer in Rust as a parallel path. Same building data, GPU-native rendering with real materials and shadows. deck.gl is the primary demo, Bevy is the performance path for when we scale to 49K photos and full temporal animation. Two renderers, one data contract.

4. Hardware Cheat Sheet

  • Box: Acer GN100 / NVIDIA DGX Spark
  • Chip: GB10 Grace Blackwell Superchip (1 Grace CPU + 1 Blackwell GPU, single die)
  • Memory: 128 GB unified (CPU+GPU share one pool, no PCIe bottleneck)
  • Model: Nemotron-3 Nano 30B A3B, Q8_K_XL GGUF, all 99 layers on GPU
  • Runtime: llama-server (llama.cpp) on :8090, CUDA 12.8, cuBLAS
  • Orchestrator: FastAPI on :30001 (real) / :30000 (stub fallback)
  • Frontend: deck.gl + maplibre-gl, serves from file:// or python3 http.server
  • Data: all on local SSD, zero network at runtime
  • Bevy/Rust: native renderer on renderer-rust-v2 branch. Bevy 0.18.1 + wgpu. Same data/landmarks-infrastructure.json. Runs natively on the GN100.

7. Philosophy / Greater Good

  • Every city has a memory. Most of it lives in filing cabinets, microfiche, and the heads of old-timers who are dying.
  • Digitization without intelligence is just storage. A scanned demolition record is useless until a model can connect it to the census block it displaced, the avenue that absorbed the families, and the cultural movement that emerged from the density.
  • The Mamdani administration is reshaping NYC urban policy. We want to put this tool in the mayor's office. Before you decide what a neighborhood becomes, you should know what it was. Sixth Borough is the institutional memory that cities lose when they optimize for the future. This is a real pitch -- not hypothetical.
  • This isn't nostalgia. Nostalgia is passive. This is forensic. Every biography is sourced from public records. Every displacement is traced from DOB demolition permits and ACS census data. The machine doesn't remember fondly. It remembers precisely.
  • The metaphysical claim: a city's memory is as real as its infrastructure. Water mains, power grids, transit networks are physical. Cultural memory is the invisible layer. Sixth Borough makes it visible, queryable, and local. A sixth sense for the city.

8. Tough Questions

Q: "How is this different from Google Maps / StreetView?"
Google shows you what's there now. We show you what was there, when it disappeared, who was displaced, and what emerged from the displacement. Temporal + cultural layer, not spatial.
Q: "Why not just use ChatGPT / cloud?"
Rule 1.6. Tenant attorneys can't upload privileged case files to OpenAI. Insurance underwriters can't send proprietary risk models to the cloud. Local inference isn't ideology, it's compliance.
Q: "Can this scale beyond NYC?"
Every US city has the same data sources (DOB permits, census, PLUTO equivalents). The engine is city-agnostic. NYC is the hardest case (1M+ buildings). If it works here, it works anywhere.
Q: "What about the Bevy/Rust renderer?"
Carson built a native Bevy renderer as a parallel path. Same JSON data, GPU-native rendering. deck.gl is the browser demo you're seeing. The Rust path is the production renderer -- when we scale to 49K photos and full temporal animation, we need native GPU performance, not WebGL. Two renderers, one data contract.
Q: "Why should this be a unicorn?"
Three compliance-locked verticals, each worth billions in annual spend. Legal aid alone is $12M/yr in NYC. We're not competing with SaaS tools -- we're replacing manual labor that legally cannot be automated by cloud AI. The box is the moat.
