## Structure (memorize this, not the words)

| Beat | What it does | Why it lands for Antler |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The product (one sentence) | Names the thing | "Discovery layer for cultural depth" — not a clone of any pitch they've heard |
| 2. The wedge (concrete) | Hip-hop heads, Bronx, 1520 Sedgwick | Specific, vivid, NYC, mythic. Antler partners who grew up in NYC will lean in. |
| 3. The expansion path | Every subculture is a new lens | Shows you understand SaaS expansion logic |
| 4. The defensibility | Local-first hardware story for privacy + offline + B2B form factors | Antler likes hardware-software fusion stories |
| 5. The Spark Story | Why the demo runs locally | Bridges the hackathon judging context to the unicorn pitch |
| 6. The team | Three people, named roles, prior projects | Antler invests in *teams and theses* — show you have both |

## What Antler is actually scoring (best guess)

Antler wants to see:
1. **A founder who can name a wedge.** Not "we're for everyone." A specific first audience.
2. **A founder who can explain expansion without hand-waving.** "Hip-hop first, then queer Village, then salsa, then jazz" is concrete. "We'll go viral and grow to 10M users" is not.
3. **A founder who can explain defensibility.** What's the moat? Cultural intelligence graph + local hardware form-factor + niche community trust = three sources of moat. Name them.
4. **A founder who's already shipping.** The hackathon weekend IS the proof of shipping. Don't apologize for the rough state — say "we're building the wedge live this weekend, want to come see it at the Hack Fair Sunday afternoon?"
5. **A team they can imagine joining the Antler residency together.** The three-person breakdown matters. Alex / Carson / James as a unit is more interesting than any one of you solo.

## Things to NOT say

- "We use Claude / GPT to..." — instant credibility loss in this room. The pitch is local-first.
- "It's like Google Earth but for history" — analogies dilute originality. Use the cultural-depth + niche-circles framing instead.
- "We're going to get $100B" — Antler partners hate spreadsheet projections at the seed stage. Lead with the wedge, not the TAM.
- "We need funding to do X" — don't ask for money in this conversation. The Antler bounty asks "is this a unicorn?" not "how much do you need?" Land the unicorn case first; funding talk is a follow-up.
- Anything about Sixth Borough being a "side project" — it's not, today. Say it like it's the main thing.

## Things to ASK Wouter (or any Antler partner) at the end

If they engage past the 90 seconds, you've earned 1-2 questions back. Use them well:

1. "Of the founders Antler has backed who started with a wedge subculture, which one expanded best — and what was the first signal that the expansion was working?"
2. "What's the most under-rated aspect of the Founder in Residence experience that I should know about as someone who already submitted an application?"

Both questions:
- Show you're already thinking like a portfolio company, not a supplicant
- Give the partner a chance to talk (people remember conversations where they were asked questions, not pitched at)
- Give you intel you actually need

> "I'll send you a link to the live demo and the architecture writeup tonight or tomorrow. If you want to come by the Hack Fair Sunday afternoon I'll be at the Sixth Borough station — would love your feedback on whether the wedge is sharp enough."

Then actually do it. Send the link the same day. Antler partners get pitched by 100 founders a week — the ones who actually follow through with what they said they'd send are the ones who get remembered.
